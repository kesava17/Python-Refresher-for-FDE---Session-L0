## My work

### DVF assessment: Desirability, Viability, Feasibility

**Engagement:** Sunrise Electronics, support deflection
**Method:** a decision passes only if it clears all three lenses. Any single red is a stop. DVF is the FDE's three hats (field, product, engineering) written as a decision lens.

#### Support deflection

| Lens | Question | Verdict | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Desirability** (field) | Do users want it and will they adopt it? | **Green** | Customers want instant answers; agents want the routine load gone. Both jobs point the same way (`../02-frame/jtbd.md`). |
| **Viability** (product) | Does it make business sense? | **Green** | ~1,200 deflectable tickets/week; payback under four months (`business-case.md`). |
| **Feasibility** (engineering) | Can we build it, with the data, risk, and time? | **Green, one amber** | Standard grounding shape; content exists; low harm with escalation. Amber: tribal knowledge is a gap (`../03-discover/discovery-brief.md`). |

**Result: GO.** Three greens and a managed amber. The amber constrains scope; it does not sink the build.

#### Contrast: the upsell idea (A2)

| Lens | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desirability | Weak | Selling inside a support answer erodes trust; customers did not ask to be sold to mid-problem |
| Viability | Weak | No success measure yet; risk of a worse experience offsets speculative revenue |
| Feasibility | Possible | Buildable, but that is only one lens |

Upsell never clears desirability or viability, and no amount of feasibility rescues that. DVF is what lets you decline it on evidence rather than preference.

_Note to self: the amber on feasibility is real but small. I flagged tribal knowledge as a scope boundary, not a blocker, so the GO still stands._
